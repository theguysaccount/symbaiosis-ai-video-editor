Two ways to go vertical

Fill (crop) keeps a vertical slice of the picture at full height. A 1920×1080 recording keeps a slice about 608 pixels wide, which is then scaled up to 1080×1920. Use the crop position slider to keep the speaker in the slice. Best for one person talking to the camera.

Fit, blurred edges shows the whole wide picture across the middle of the screen, over a blurred, darkened copy of it. Nothing is cut off. Best for screen recordings, two people side by side, or anything where the edges matter.

Videos are exported as MP4 (H.264 video, AAC sound) at your recording's own frame rate, up to 60 fps, which every platform accepts. If your browser can't encode H.264, it exports WebM instead.

Before you crop

  • Framing matters more than resolution: a centred speaker survives a 9:16 crop; someone standing at the edge of a wide shot doesn't.
  • Captions go lower-middle in vertical video, above the app's buttons. The Captions tab's “Lower” position is set for that.
  • If you'll publish both a wide and a vertical version, export twice: once as Original, once as 9:16.

Questions

Will the quality drop when I crop?

Cropping a 1080p wide video to 9:16 enlarges the kept slice by about 1.8 times, so it's a little softer than the original. Recording in 4K gives a sharp vertical crop. Fit with blurred edges doesn't enlarge anything.

Can it follow the speaker automatically?

Not yet: you set the crop position once for the whole video. For most talking-head recordings the speaker stays in one place, so one position is enough.

Does it work without cutting anything?

Yes. Choose “Keep all” pauses and untick filler words in the Cut tab, and untick captions, and it simply converts the shape.
