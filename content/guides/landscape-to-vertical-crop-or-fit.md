A wide video and a vertical screen meet in one of two ways: you cut the sides off, or you shrink the whole picture to fit. Both are one setting in the Format tab; the right one depends on what's in your shot.

## Crop (fill the frame)

Cropping keeps a vertical slice of the full height. From a 1920×1080 recording, a 9:16 slice is about 608 pixels wide, scaled up to 1080×1920 for export.

**Use it when** one person is talking to the camera, roughly centred. The face fills the phone screen, which is what holds attention on short-form.

**Watch out for:**

- **Softness.** The slice is enlarged about 1.8 times. It's acceptable from 1080p and sharp from 4K.
- **Framing.** Anyone standing off-centre may be cut off. Use the crop position slider to move the slice; it's set once for the whole video.
- **On-screen text.** Lower thirds and slides at the edges of a wide shot will be lost.

## Fit with blurred edges

Fitting shows the entire wide picture across the middle of the vertical frame, over an enlarged, blurred and darkened copy of itself, so there are no black bars.

**Use it when** the edges matter: screen recordings, two people side by side, wide scenery, slides.

**Watch out for:** the picture occupies only about a third of the screen's height, so faces are small. Put captions in the blurred area (Top or Lower) so they don't cover the picture.

## Rule of thumb

If you'd describe the shot as "a person talking", crop. If you'd describe it as "a thing being shown", fit.

## Record for both

If you know you'll publish vertically, frame wide recordings with the speaker in the centre third and keep important text away from the sides. Then one recording serves YouTube (export as Original) and Shorts (export as 9:16, cropped) with no compromises.
