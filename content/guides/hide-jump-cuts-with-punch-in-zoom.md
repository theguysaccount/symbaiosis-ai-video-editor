Cut a pause out of a single-camera recording and the picture jumps: the speaker's head is in a slightly different place before and after. Editors call it a jump cut. On TikTok and Reels nobody minds. In a polished YouTube video, they can make the edit feel rough.

## The trick

Editors with one camera fake a second one. Every other section is framed slightly tighter, typically 10 to 20% closer. Now each cut reads as a change of camera angle rather than a glitch, because the eye expects things to shift when the framing changes.

In Symbaiosis, tick **Punch-in zoom on every other cut** in the Format tab. Kept sections alternate between normal framing and 12% closer. It applies to the preview immediately, so you can judge it before exporting.

## When it helps

- Talking heads with many cuts close together, where the jumps are frequent.
- Wide shots cropped to vertical: the zoom also varies a static frame.
- Explainers where you want a "produced" feel.

## When to skip it

- **Very tight crops.** If a 9:16 crop already enlarges a 1080p video, another 12% softens the image further.
- **Screen recordings.** Zooming in on interface text makes it jump around and blur.
- **Few cuts.** With one or two cuts a minute, the occasional jump isn't worth the zoom.

## Other ways to cover a cut

- **B-roll:** cover the cut with a shot of what you're talking about. That needs a main editor; export the trimmed video from Symbaiosis first, then add b-roll on top.
- **Keep the pause:** sometimes the cleanest fix is not to cut. Tap the pause's ⏸ chip to keep it.
