Many people scroll with the sound off, so on short-form video captions aren't an accessibility extra: they're how most people first meet your words. Good captions are read without effort. Bad ones fight the picture.

## How many words at a time

Fewer words on screen means bigger text and a faster rhythm that matches speech:

- **One word:** maximum energy, very large text. Good for punchy hooks and short clips, tiring over a minute.
- **Up to three words:** the common short-form style. Big enough to read at a glance; each caption is a phrase.
- **Up to six words:** for wide videos and longer talks, where viewers expect to read in sentences.

Symbaiosis breaks captions at sentence ends and at pauses, so a caption never strands the first word of the next sentence.

## Size and weight

Text on a phone needs to be readable at arm's length on a small screen. Use a heavy weight (the editor uses Montserrat ExtraBold) and keep caption height around 6 to 8% of the frame's width. Thin fonts and small sizes disappear over moving footage.

## Contrast

White text alone vanishes over bright backgrounds. Three reliable fixes:

1. A thick dark outline (the **Bold pop** style).
2. A dark box behind the text (**Boxed**), the most legible over busy footage.
3. A soft shadow (**Clean**), subtle and fine over darker scenes.

Highlighting the word being spoken, in yellow or another bright colour, helps the eye keep its place.

## Position

On TikTok, Reels and Shorts, the bottom of the screen is covered by the post's own caption and the app's buttons, and the right edge holds the like and share buttons. Captions placed around 65 to 75% of the way down, centred and not too wide, stay clear of both. That's where the **Lower** position puts them. **Middle** is the safe choice if your clip has text near the bottom.

## Uppercase and punctuation

Uppercase captions look bold and uniform, a common short-form choice. Hiding commas and full stops removes visual noise; question and exclamation marks stay because they change meaning. Both are options in the Captions tab.

## Always proofread

A misheard word in big yellow letters is the first thing viewers comment on. Read the transcript once before exporting and fix names and jargon with "Fix text".
